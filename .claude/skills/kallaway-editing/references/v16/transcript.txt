This company built a data center floating in the Pacific Ocean. They're called Pantalassa, and they might be pioneering a whole new category of data center technology. See, a data center is really just thousands of computer chips that only need two things, electricity to think and water to cool. On land, they compete with actual towns for both of those resources, which is why these projects keep getting blocked. But the ocean has no towns, no permits, and no grids to constrain. Of course, there is just one problem with an ocean -based data center. There are no plugs to plug in for electricity. So this floating data center was designed to make its own power. Here's how it works. Picture a giant floating lollipop in the sea with a round buoy on top and a long tube underneath. Waves only move at the top of the surface, so underneath it's actually very calm. And that means the buoy rides up and down while the tube stays still. Every wave that passes by squeezes seawater through its herbide. Think of it like a bike pump, except the ocean waves is what's pushing the handle up and down. That same seawater is also great for cooling the chips in the data center, so it accomplishes both tasks required. That you didn't calm weather and no waves in the ocean would prevent this from running. But it actually works a lot more like a battery. The sun heats the air, the air makes wind, and the wind pushes the waves. And those waves keep rolling long after the breeze stops. So it's solar power that also works at night. But there's just one more problem. How do they get all that power back to the land to use? And it turns out they don't. These things have no anchor, no fuel, and no cable to shore. Instead, your AI query hits them, goes straight up to a satellite, and then goes straight back down to the data center. The real problem making this tough is that the ocean typically destroys everything. The salt eats metal, the barnacles grow out everything, and storms do what storms do. And that's really the bet that Panthalass is making, that they can figure out how to make this in a way the ocean can't destroy it. The first full -size versions of this go live in the ocean later this year. If they survive, your AI queries might start being powered by a floating data center in the ocean.
