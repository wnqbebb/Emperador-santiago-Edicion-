Claude just performed brain surgery on a living animal for the first time ever. If you'd like technology, this is one of the wildest stories I've heard in a while. Now the goal of the surgery was very simple. Figure out how to reduce seizures in a mouse that had epilepsy. The startup behind this is called Exyn Therapeutics. And what they did with Claude was super interesting. Exyn gave Claude a list of 19 possible gene treatments, as well as control over robotic arm system with a needle and a camera. Now nobody told Claude what treatment to pick or how to execute the operation. The only instructions they gave it were to reduce the seizures. Epilepsy causes too many of the brain's neurons to get overly excited at the same time. So Claude decided to use a gene therapy that would boost the brain's natural calming signal. And it even compared three different cases on the topic to figure out the exact right spot to do the injection. Now a real human vet opened the skull and had a kill switch in case anything went wrong, but Claude executed the operation perfectly. And the cool part is it was extremely careful. It kept checking the camera over and over to make sure there wasn't extra bleeding and the operation went well. Now this may seem like a silly story, but I think it marks an incredible milestone in the progress of AI, especially in the science and medical fields. A lot of us forget, chatGPT came out less than four years ago. And now we're seeing general purpose LLMs conducting live surgery on a real animal. The pace of progress in science and medicine is absolutely insane.
